Field Identification Guide

How to Spot an Asian Tiger Mosquito

Before a community can control an invasive species, residents need to recognize it. Unlike dull brown native mosquitoes that buzz around at night, Aedes albopictus has unmistakable high-contrast markings and bold daytime behavior:

  • 1 Single Silver-White Dorsal Stripe: One crisp white line runs straight down the center of its jet-black back (thorax) and head.
  • 2 Black-and-White Banded Legs: Bright white rings wrap around its dark legs—giving it the "tiger" nickname.
  • 3 Small Size (~1/4 inch / 2–10 mm): Smaller than many native marsh mosquitoes, making it agile and stealthy.
  • 4 Aggressive Daytime Biter: Hunts all day long—especially early morning and late afternoon—often targeting ankles and elbows.
Anatomical ID Blueprint Scale: ~1/4 in (6 mm)

🇺🇸 U.S. Invasion History

From One Tire Shipment to 40+ States

Native to Southeast Asia, Aedes albopictus was first discovered established in the continental U.S. in Houston, Texas in 1985 inside imported used tires that held rainwater and dormant eggs. Because highways moved used tires and potted plants across the country—and because its eggs can survive freezing winters in diapause—it rapidly colonized more than 40 U.S. states, thriving across the East Coast, Midwest, and South.

Why Suburbs Are Its Favorite Habitat:

Shaded patios, kids' toys, rain barrels, and corrugated downspout pipes create thousands of predator-free "mini-ponds" per square mile—with zero fish or frogs to eat the larvae.

🩺 Public Health Impact

More Than Just an Itchy Backyard Nuisance

In addition to ruining outdoor play and sports during daylight hours, the Asian tiger mosquito is a competent disease vector—meaning it can pick up and spread serious viruses and parasites to both people and pets:

🦠 Dengue & Zika Viruses
🦠 Chikungunya Virus
🧠 West Nile & La Crosse
🐾 Dog Heartworm

Because it bites both humans and animals, scientists call it a "bridge vector" capable of moving diseases between wildlife and neighborhoods.

Version 1 • Initial Prototype

What We Built First (And What Went Wrong)

  • Open-top 32 oz matte-black plastic cup with water, oak leaf attractant, and quarter Bti Mosquito Dunk.
  • Two bare jumper wires taped inside the cup connected to a micro:bit moisture alarm.
  • Field Feedback Flaw #1: Heavy rain could fill the cup to the rim and short-circuit the electronics.
  • Field Feedback Flaw #2: Squirrels, dogs, and falling autumn leaves could disturb the open water.
Version 2 • Iterated Prototype

How Feedback Made Our Design Stronger

  • Drilled Two 1/4-Inch Overflow Holes 1.5 inches below the rim so rain drains safely below the electronics.
  • Added Slotted Mesh Lid that lets mosquitoes enter while blocking squirrels, pets, and leaves.
  • Added 30-Day Bti Timer & Amber LED so homeowners know exactly when to swap the Bti puck.
  • Added Lid QR Code Sticker linking neighbors to a quick backyard hotspot reporting form.
